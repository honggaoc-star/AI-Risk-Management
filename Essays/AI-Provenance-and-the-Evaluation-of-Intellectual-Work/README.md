# AI Provenance and the Evaluation of Intellectual Work: Rational Risk, Social Meaning, and Human–AI Performance

**Version:** 1.0<br>
**Date:** September 2026<br>
**Author:** Honggao Cao

This paper connects research on human–AI performance with research on AI disclosure and provenance in intellectual work. It argues that the relevant performance benchmark follows from the evaluative objective and that a binary “AI was used” label is often informationally sparse. Function, retained human authority, verification, and the evaluative target can be more informative. The paper distinguishes statistical-risk, institutional-purpose, and social-meaning explanations for provenance effects, treats them as explanations to test rather than mechanically decompose, and recognizes that anticipated disclosure can change production behavior before evaluation occurs and that the meaning and value of provenance can evolve over time.

## Relationship to the AIRM Program

The paper addresses provenance, disclosure, human–AI performance, evaluation, institutional decision-making, and governance questions relevant to AI Risk Management. It remains a reader-facing essay/paper; placement in this repository does not by itself promote it into an active research initiative.
