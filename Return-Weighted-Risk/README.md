# Return-Weighted Risk for Navigating an Evolving AI Landscape

This directory is the home of a research initiative within AI Risk Management.

The project examines a practical governance problem: organizations increasingly undertake AI activities while still learning what value those activities create, what resources they require, and what risks they introduce. Rapid changes in capabilities, configurations, user behavior, organizational purpose, and feasible alternatives can make point-in-time assessments short-lived.

**Return-Weighted Risk (RWR)** is an activity-level governance approach that keeps an evolving value case connected to an independently assessed risk case. It organizes continuing authorization around three sequential tests: **admissibility**, **risk permissibility**, and **value justification**. Intended value can direct additional risk inquiry without determining the independent risk conclusion; feasible alternatives remain relevant after initial approval; and material change can reopen the authorization decision.

The central question is straightforward: **given what the organization knows now about value and risk, does the evidence still support the same action?**

## Status

Version 1.1 is the accepted working-paper version as of August 2026. The core manuscript project is closed for now. Subsequent public-sharing, repository, announcement, or dissemination activity does not by itself reopen the manuscript.

The paper uses a **hypothetical small-business-lending example** to illustrate how RWR can change a continuing authorization decision as evidence develops. The example is illustrative rather than empirical evidence for the effectiveness of RWR. Empirical validation remains future work.

## Scope and Boundaries

RWR is a governance approach, not a formula for converting value and risk into a common score. It does not replace existing risk management, benefits-realization, or AI-governance practices. Its focus is the activity-level authorization decision at which the current value case and independently assessed risk case must support the proposed action.

This initiative is exploratory research. It does not certify that an AI model, system, workflow, organization, or use is safe, compliant, accurate, aligned, or fit for a particular purpose.
